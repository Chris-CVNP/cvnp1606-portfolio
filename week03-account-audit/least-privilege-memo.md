The principle of least privilege states that people should have only as much authority as they need to do their jobs. Therefore, local administrator access should only be permitted in circumstances that reflect either a valid business necessity or technical justification that could not otherwise be accomplished using Standard User permissions.

When Does Local Administrator Access Make Sense?
There are several types of employees whose regular duties involve higher levels of authorization. These might include IT professionals, workstation technicians, or developers. However, such access is limited to those areas and devices where it is actually required and should not exceed the scope of the employee's authorized activities. Convenience is never a justifiable reason for granting local admin rights. No employee should receive local administrator rights merely so that he/she can easily install applications or modify system configurations.

When Should a Request Be Escalated?
An escalation process should occur if you believe the business case has not been clearly articulated, the degree of authority being requested appears excessive relative to the individual's position, the request will conflict with one or more established security policies at ACME, etc. Requests for access to sensitive systems, non-standard software, security mechanisms or processes, or beyond the limits of an employee's normal duties should be subject to further evaluation. Escalating the process affords the opportunity for an appropriately authorized supervisor, system owner, or member of the security team to assess whether the increased authority is warranted and if a less risky method exists to complete the required tasks.

Documentation Supporting Exceptions:
If I were to grant someone local admin rights for an event that I believed was reasonable but not routine, I would expect them to provide me documentation supporting the exception. The documentation would describe the specific activity requiring administrator privileges, why Standard User permissions are inadequate, which computers and systems are impacted, and how long the permission would be required. I would also expect both managerial approval and/or system owner approval depending on the scope and nature of the request. When applicable, I would also recommend that an IT or security representative evaluate the request. Any software involved should be identified along with explanations regarding why it requires administrator level access. Depending on the duration of access, a weekly or biweekly audit of the work done and the employee's system logs should be conducted. In all cases, exceptions should be time limited and specific to the particular device or task, and once the work is completed, the local admin status should be removed.
